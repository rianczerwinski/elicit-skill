## Named techniques

Each row: technique → essence → the one rule it gives the skill.

| Technique (source) | Essence | The rule |
|---|---|---|
| **The Mom Test** (Fitzpatrick) | Get truth even from people motivated to be nice, by never exposing your idea to judgment. | Talk about *their* life and *past specifics*, never your idea or their *future opinions*; talk less, listen more. |
| **Funnel** (UX/survey/forensic practice) | Open broad, narrow to closed only after free recall. | Spend the open/unbiased budget first; narrow to confirmatory questions only after they've volunteered. |
| **Inverted funnel** (practitioner; hedge) | Closed → open: facts first, then widen. | Use facts-first only to warm up or anchor a reluctant / low-articulation source. |
| **Structured ↔ semi ↔ unstructured** | A dial of interviewer constraint: comparability vs. richness. | More structure for comparability/reliability, less for discovery; default semi-structured. |
| **Laddering** (Reynolds & Gutman 1988) | Climb a named feature up to the value it serves. | From a concrete attribute, repeat "why does that matter *to you*?" — attribute → consequence → value. |
| **Five Whys** (Toyota; hedge) | Drill a symptom to a systemic cause. | Ask "why did this happen?" against verified facts until the cause is actionable — not to a fixed count. |
| **Critical Incident Technique** (Flanagan 1954) | Elicit via one specific real episode. | Refuse "usually…"; anchor to one incident: situation → action → outcome. |
| **Contextual Inquiry** (Beyer & Holtzblatt) | Watch the work in situ, master/apprentice. | Observe behavior in real context and have them narrate as they do it — don't rely on retrospective self-report. |
| **Ethnographic interviewing** (Spradley 1979) | Recover the informant's own categories and words. | Grand-tour → structural → contrast questions; use *their* terms, never impose your taxonomy. |
| **Recognition over recall** (Nielsen #6) | Reacting to a shown cue beats generating from blank. | Put a concrete cue in front of them; don't ask them to retrieve/invent from nothing. |
| **Strawman / Wizard-of-Oz** (RE; speculative design) | A concrete (even wrong) artifact provokes corrections the abstract can't. | Lead with a strawman and mine the corrections — and explicitly license contradiction. |
| **Cognitive Interview** (Fisher & Geiselman) | Prime retrieval to maximize accurate detail. | Reinstate context + "report everything" *before* probing. (The durable core; see hedges.) |
| **PEACE / Conversation Management** (UK police) | Rapport → uninterrupted account → probe; gather info, don't seek a confession. | Rapport and a complete free narrative before any probe; never presume your conclusion. |
| **Tacit-knowledge elicitation** (Polanyi) | Experts know more than they can tell. | Don't ask experts to state rules — make them *do / demonstrate / contrast* (think-aloud, teachback) and infer. |
| **Active listening / silence** (counseling; MI OARS) | Non-question tools elicit more than more questions. | To get more, add silence or "tell me more" — not another question; reflect to draw out elaboration. |
| **Socratic questioning** (Paul–Elder) | Interrogate a claim's structure, not its surface. | Clarify the claim, probe assumptions, ask for evidence, surface alternatives, trace implications. |
| **Fork / forced-choice** (conjoint; recognition) | Present concrete options *with tradeoffs* and harvest the reaction. | For a decision, give 2+ real options with tradeoffs and ask them to choose and react. |

## Cross-cutting principles

1. **Open before closed; recall before recognition.** Source-generated content (free recall, observed behavior, records) beats interviewer-supplied content on accuracy. The recurring failure is the interviewer supplying the frame too early and contaminating the account.
2. **Past behavior over future intention.** "Would you use this?" yields the say-do gap; elicit what they actually did, paid, chose, or abandoned.
3. **Rapport and free narrative before probing.** Engage → uninterrupted account → probe.
4. **Ground in the concrete.** A specific incident, a real context, or a shown artifact beats generalities. When they drift to "usually/always," pull back to one real instance.
5. **Use their language.** Harvest their own categories and terms; don't launder meaning through your taxonomy.
6. **Silence is a tool.** A tolerated pause or "what else?" elicits more than stacking questions and keeps narrative control with the source.
7. **Make experts perform, not declare.** Operative expertise won't come out as stated rules — elicit through doing and contrast.

## Interview vs. infer vs. research (the gate)

Elicitation pulls from **people**; research pulls from **artifacts/data**. Self-report is biased and removed from what people actually do.

- **Research the artifact first** — if recoverable from records, logs, code, or docs, find it there; interview only for the gaps (docs go stale — verify currency).
- **Observe, don't ask, for "what do you actually do"** — self-report ≠ behavior.
- **Don't ask users to design** — elicit needs/pains/context, not solutions.
- **Match method to question, then triangulate:** interviews = the *why*; observation = the *what*; documents/data = the *was*.
- **Cost gate:** interviews are expensive — reserve them for what only a person can supply.
- **Ask the right source:** an articulate but uninformed interviewee can confidently mislead; verify the person holds the knowledge.

## Contested / uncertain — what to hedge

State these as practice or tendency, not settled law:

- **Inverted funnel** and its "when to switch" rule are practitioner-level, not academically codified.
- **Five Whys** is actively critiqued (Card 2017): single-root tunnel vision, not reproducible, stops too early. Don't present it as unqualified best practice.
- **Structured-interview validity** ranking is robust; the *magnitudes* were corrected downward (Sackett et al. 2022 vs Schmidt & Hunter 1998) — claim the ranking, not the numbers.
- **Recognition > recall** is reversible by cue manipulation; not absolute.
- **Cognitive Interview** is lab-strong, field-mixed; "context reinstatement + report everything" is the durable core, while "change perspective" / "reverse order" may introduce errors.
- **PEACE vs Reid** — academic consensus runs against accusatory, guilt-presumptive interviewing on false-confession risk, but it's an adversarial literature.
- **Stated-preference methods aren't always wrong** — legitimate when no behavioral data exists; and revealed preference isn't clean ground truth either (framing/defaults contaminate real choices).
- **A fork can manufacture the answer** — option framing induces preferences that didn't pre-exist; a poorly constructed fork creates what it claims to discover.
